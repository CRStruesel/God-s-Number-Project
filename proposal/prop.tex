\title{Proposal: AI powered experiments on $SL_2(\Int_p)$}
\author{Benjamin Krawciw}
\author{Cody Strouse}
\author{Jake Quinn}
\anthor{Wenzhuo Wu}
\maketitle


$\slzp$ has two generators, $S$ and $T$. Given any $\gamma$ in $\slzp$ we can consider how many applications of $S$ and $T$ are needed to reach it. There is a smallest such number, call it $\ell(\gamma)$, and as $\slzp$ is finite, there exists some $\gamma$ in the group such that $\ell(\gamma)$ is greater than or equal to any other $\ell(\alpha), \alpha \in \slzp$. This value is known as the diameter of the group, or informally, God's number. Denote God's number by $G(\slzp)$. 


It is known that $\Gs = O(\log(p))$, but the constant is not known. Also, God's numbers for $\slzp$ are not usually computed or tabulated. By computing $\Gs$ for many small $p$, we find an experimental estimate for C.

Cayley graphs for $\slzp$ equipped with the standard generators are Ramanujan graphs, which are known for their incredibly rapid growth. By computing $\slzp$, we are finding the radius of such graphs.
