\author{Benjamin Krawciw, Cody Strouse, Jake Quinn, Wenzhuo Wu}
\maketitle

$\slzp$ has two generators, $S$ and $T$. Given any $\gamma$ in $\slzp$ we can consider how many applications of $S$ and $T$ are needed to reach it. There is a smallest such number, call it $\ell(\gamma)$, and as $\slzp$ is finite, there exists some $\gamma$ in the group such that $\ell(\gamma)$ is greater than or equal to any other $\ell(\alpha), \alpha \in \slzp$. This value is known as the diameter of the group, or informally, God's number. Denote God's number by $G(\slzp)$ \cite{helfgott2008growthgenerationsl2zpz}.

We propose using AI assisted programming to explore the diameter of the group $\slzp$. $\slzp$ has two generators, $S$ and $T$. It is known that $\Gs = O(\log(p))$, but the constant is not known. Also, God's numbers for $\slzp$ are not usually computed or tabulated. By computing $\Gs$ for many small $p$, we find an experimental estimate for C.

Cayley graphs for $\slzp$ equipped with the standard generators are Ramanujan graphs, which are known for their incredibly rapid growth \cite{lubotzkyphillipssarnak}. By computing $\slzp$, we are finding the radius of such graphs.
